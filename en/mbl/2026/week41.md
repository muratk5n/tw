AM is low frequency radio obviously, can transmit over long distances,
a single powerful AM transmitter can cover entire multi-state regions.

Tech dictates the economics - few stations meant centralized, national
mass culture. AM towers cost a fortune to run and reached massive
audiences, station owners couldn't afford to take risks, programmers
invented the "Top 40" format in the 1950s, playing the same 40 catchy,
safe, highly commercial hit songs on repeat all day long. The Beatles
fit that mold, upbeat, tightly structured 3-minute pop songs as if
precision-engineered for mass appeal on AM.

FM signals use higher frequencies, cannot travel long distances,
easily blocked by hills and tall buildings. That means each locality
could have its own set of FM stations, but smaller local FM station
cannot compete with the massive marketing budgets of established AM
players, they catered to smaller, localized, counter-cultural, or
college-aged audiences. No tight, squeaky-clean AM pop mold, you get
The Doors, Jimi Hendrix, Steely Dan.

"Following a recession, which happen about every 7 years, investors
will likely be cautious and will only borrow so much that they can
still repay their loan: what Minsky the ‘hedge’ phase. Cautious
investors are more likely to see returns, albeit low, and this will
lead them to extend themselves further over time until they enter what
Minsky called the ‘speculative’ phase. At this point borrowing
increases, asset prices start to rise, investors enjoy healthy returns
and exuberance builds until they enter the 'Ponzi' phase, where they
can only finance their increasing debt repayments with further debt or
by selling off assets in a market where prices are continually
rising. Eventually scepticism prevails and the bubble bursts."
