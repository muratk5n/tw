Mathematician Steven Strogatz: "We have these two cultures. I mean we have many pairs of cultures. One pair is problem solvers versus theory builders. Theory builder means a person who invents new concepts of in math and uses those concepts to that that are like new theoretical apparatus, new ways of looking at old questions. Powerful theory can lead to making what used to seem like intractable problems suddenly become easy, solvable or approachable at the minimum.

There was a mathematician in the 20th century named Alexander Grothendieck who is often seen as the maybe the best mathematician of the century. And Grothendieck had this analogy of a hard problem. Picture a really hard nut that you're trying to crack. One approach would be to take out a hammer and start whacking with a chisel. That's one approach to problem solving. Another approach that Grothendieck advocated was to put the nut in a surrounding bath of warm water and let the liquid start to seep in there. You see where I'm going. When you make the nut soft enough, you could just open it up.

And for him, developing the right theory was like the warm water whereas brute force problem solving is like the chisel. So Grothendieck advocated thinking deeply and creating and building new theories. This is where Alex [Whistner Gross'] comment is wrong. When he says that AI will solve math, maybe yes in the chisel sense, but so far it has shown no aptitude for building theory. It hasn't put together concepts or invented new concepts that have made problems easier.. They haven't done it yet. They haven't even hinted at doing it yet"
