Note that neural machine translation (NMT) came *first*, before
LLM. According to their lore, when Bengio was sending grad students to
Kyunghyun Cho's way in 2014 so they would help him try out different
approaches (and one of them [co]invented the critical Attention
approach), they were focused on NMT. Cho went to University of
Montréal having decided already "I want to work on NMT".  The later
Google Attention paper was about NMT, the approach was later adapted
for chat. Ergo all that Query, Key, and Value (QKV) business of LLMs
is also in NMTs, like the one Firefox uses.

Mathematician Steven Strogatz: "We have these two cultures. I mean we
have many pairs of cultures. One pair is problem solvers versus theory
builders. Theory builder means a person who invents new concepts of in
math and uses those concepts to that that are like new theoretical
apparatus, new ways of looking at old questions. Powerful theory can
lead to making what used to seem like intractable problems suddenly
become easy, solvable or approachable at the minimum.

There was a mathematician in the 20th century named Alexander
Grothendieck who is often seen as the maybe the best mathematician of
the century. And Grothendieck had this analogy of a hard
problem. Picture a really hard nut that you're trying to crack. One
approach would be to take out a hammer and start whacking with a
chisel. That's one approach to problem solving. Another approach that
Grothendieck advocated was to put the nut in a surrounding bath of
warm water and let the liquid start to seep in there. You see where
I'm going. When you make the nut soft enough, you could just open it
up.

And for him, developing the right theory was like the warm water
whereas brute force problem solving is like the chisel. So
Grothendieck advocated thinking deeply and creating and building new
theories. This is where Alex [Whistner Gross'] comment is wrong. When
he says that AI will solve math, maybe yes in the chisel sense, but so
far it has shown no aptitude for building theory. It hasn't put
together concepts or invented new concepts that have made problems
easier.. They haven't done it yet. They haven't even hinted at doing
it yet"
