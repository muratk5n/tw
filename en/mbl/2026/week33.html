Chaos, probability.. yet underneath, determinism. This is the way.

The entire subject of chaos is deterministic, the unpredictability comes from the fact that tiniest changes in the initial condition can cause wild swings 10-20 steps later (due to non-linearity) which seems chaotic, and at a large view you could view that probabilisticaly as well. The famous Lorenz equation plotted in 3D has that butterfly shape right? Well you could ask what is the the probability of being at one of those wings? It turns out exactly 0.5 (50%).

Classical QM is probabilistic only at certain points (during measurement). Other times, eg the evolution of the wave function is completely deterministic.

Jacob Barandes, in his formulation, extends the probabilistic viewpoint to the entire process, not just at measurement. He took the Copenhagen interpretation to its natural conclusion.

But that still doesn't mean there is something completely deterministic going on underneath. I gave the example of gas simulation before. DSMC, LBM are probabilistic methods to compute the (deterministic) movement of gases. Underneath the movement is completely mechanistic, gas molecules can literally be assumed to behave like billiard balls in fact. We know this. We know how gases behave, but since it is too hard to run a molecular dynamics type simulation where you compute every little molecule's movement, we have to resort to probabilistic methods.
