Classical QM is probabilistic only at *certain points* (during
measurement). Other times, eg the evolution of the wave function is
completely deterministic.

Jacob Barandes, in his formulation, extends the probabilistic
viewpoint to the *entire* process, not just at measurement. He took
the Copenhagen interpretation to its natural conclusion. 

But that still doesn't mean there is something completely
deterministic going on underneath. I gave the example of gas
simulation before.  DSMC, LBM are probabilistic methods to compute the
(deterministic) movement of gases. Underneath the movement is
completely mechanistic, gas molecules can literally be assumed to
behave like billiard balls in fact. We know this. We know how gases
behave, but since it is too hard to run a *molecular dynamics* type
simulation where you compute every little molecule's movement, we have
to resort to probabilistic methods.
